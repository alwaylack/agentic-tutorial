## 7.1 What Is Chain-of-Thought

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is a prompt engineering technique that guides the model to think step by step by adding **reasoning steps** between the question and the answer, thereby improving performance on complex tasks.

Traditional prompts often go directly from question to answer:

```
Question: Xiao Ming has 5 apples, eats 2, then buys 3 more. How many does he have now?
Answer: 6
```

Chain-of-thought shows the intermediate reasoning:

```
Question: Xiao Ming has 5 apples, eats 2, then buys 3 more. How many does he have now?
Thinking:
- Started with 5 apples
- Ate 2, remaining: 5 - 2 = 3
- Bought 3 more, now: 3 + 3 = 6
Answer: 6
```

Google's team first systematically demonstrated the power of CoT in their 2022 paper: for **complex tasks** requiring multi-step computation — arithmetic, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic推理 — CoT brings significant performance gains.

## 7.2 Manual Few-shot CoT

The simplest and most effective approach is to manually write a few examples containing reasoning steps (few-shot), letting the model mimic this "show your thinking" style.

```python
prompt = """
Q: The cafeteria had 23 apples. If they used 20 to make lunch and bought 6 more, how many apples do they have?
A: They started with 23 apples, used 20 for lunch (23 - 20 = 3), then bought 6 more (3 + 6 = 9). The answer is 9.

Q: A retail store marks up a price of $80 by 50%. Then they offer a 20% discount. What is the final price?
A: Original price $80. Markup by 50%: $80 * 1.5 = $120. Discount of 20%: $120 * 0.8 = $96. The answer is $96.

Q: {question}
A:
"""
```

Key points:
- **Examples should be specific**: each example shows a clear reasoning chain
- **Maintain diversity**: examples cover different types of reasoning (arithmetic, logic, commonsense)
- **Consistent format**: use a fixed "Thinking:" prefix to help the model understand the pattern

## 7.3 Zero-Shot CoT

When you don't have enough examples, you can use Zero-shot CoT. The core trick is adding a **trigger phrase** after the question, such as:

> "Let's think step by step."

```python
# Traditional prompt (prone to errors)
prompt = "I went to the market and bought 10 apples. I gave 2 apples to my neighbor and 2 to the repairman. I then went and bought 5 more apples and ate 1. How many apples did I remain with?"

# Zero-shot CoT
prompt = """I went to the market and bought 10 apples. I gave 2 apples to my neighbor and 2 to the repairman. I then went and bought 5 more apples and ate 1. How many apples did I remain with?

Let's think step by step."""
```

Research shows that a simple "Let's think step by step" is enough to trigger the model's chain-reasoning capability, especially on larger models (e.g. GPT-3.5+).

## 7.4 Auto-CoT: Automatically Generating Reasoning Chains

For large-scale application scenarios, manually writing examples is costly. Auto-CoT proposes an automated approach:

1. **Question clustering**: split the question dataset into several clusters
2. **Representative sampling**: pick representative questions from each cluster
3. **Automatic reasoning chain generation**: use Zero-shot CoT to generate reasoning for each representative question
4. **Build example set**: use the generated reasoning chains as few-shot examples

```python
# Auto-CoT example generation flow
def auto_cot_example(question):
    base_prompt = f"{question}\nLet's think step by step."
    response = llm.complete(base_prompt)
    # Extract the reasoning chain portion
    return extract_chain_of_thought(response)
```

This approach is particularly suitable for scenarios needing大量 examples, such as benchmark evaluation or prompt optimization in production systems.

## 7.5 Applicable Scenarios and Limitations of CoT

### Applicable Scenarios
- Math computation and arithmetic reasoning
- Multi-step logical deduction
- Planning tasks requiring intermediate states
- Factual knowledge queries (reduces hallucination)

### Not Applicable Scenarios
- Simple classification tasks (e.g. sentiment analysis)
- Creative writing (may constrain divergent thinking)
- Real-time conversations (adds latency)
- Small-scale models (can be counterproductive when capability is insufficient)

### Practical Advice

```python
# Decision tree for whether to use CoT
def should_use_cot(task_type, model_size):
    if task_type in ['math', 'logic', 'multi_step']:
        return model_size >= 10  # at least 10B parameters
    elif task_type in ['classification', 'summary']:
        return False
    return model_size >= 70  # only large models are worth trying
```

## Chapter Summary

- **Manual CoT**: hand-write few-shot examples containing thinking processes
- **Zero-shot CoT**: add "Let's think step by step" trigger phrase
- **Auto-CoT**: automatically generate reasoning chains for large-scale scenarios
- **Applicability**: works well on complex reasoning tasks, may be counterproductive on simple tasks
